<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- The person on a record: an owner, a trustee, a beneficiary, an advisor.
- Who did something, in a comment, an activity feed, or an audit trail.
- Someone's availability, with `presence`, or their reply to an invitation, with `status`.
- A person people pick or filter by, as a button with `isInteractive` and `isSelected`.
- A team or partner firm among people, with `entityType="team"`.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **Several people together:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Avatar Group" />, which overlaps them and counts the rest.
- **A status or a count that isn't about a person:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Label" /> or <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Badge" />.
- **An action:** <DocLink to="Components/Actions/Icon Button" />, unless the action is picking the person.
- **A firm, trust, or account as its own identity:** Avatar is for people. `entityType="team"` only lets a team sit among them.

</UseInstead>

<Section title="Variants">

Avatar is always a circle. What changes is what's inside it, the badge, and the size.

<Subsection title="Photo or fallback">

With `src`, Avatar shows the photo, cropped to fill the circle. Without one, or when it fails to
load, it shows shared fallback artwork that follows the light or dark theme: a person, or with
`entityType="team"` a two-person silhouette for a team or a partner firm. There's no initials
version.

<Example of={AvatarStories.Variants} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Presence and status">

`presence` shows availability: `online` is a green dot, `offline` a grey ring, and `busy` red with a
bar. `status` shows a reply, such as to an invitation: `accepted` is green with a check, and
`declined` red with a cross. Avatar shows one badge at most, so when both are set, `status` wins.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Sizes">

Six sizes, from `xxs` (16px) to `xl` (72px): 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, and 72px, with `md` the default.
The badge grows with the avatar, from 8px at `xxs` to 20px at `xl`. At `xxs` and `xs` it sits 2px
outside the circle, so it stays visible on a small avatar.

<Example of={AvatarStories.Sizes} />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Interactive">

`isInteractive`, or an `onClick`, renders the avatar as a native `button`. Hover and press tint the
image with an overlay that fades in with `fade-quick`, and keyboard focus shows the shared Focus Ring
2px outside the circle. A static avatar has no hover or focus look.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Selected and disabled">

`isSelected` draws a 2px brand ring inside the edge. The ring grows in with the `move-quick` recipe,
and appears at once with reduced motion. Hover and press on a selected avatar use a brand overlay.
The ring also shows on a static avatar, so keep `isSelected` for avatars people can pick. Passing
`isSelected`, `true` or `false`, to an interactive avatar makes it a toggle button with
`aria-pressed`; leave it unset on an avatar that only opens something.

`disabled` dims the avatar to 40% opacity and drops the hover and press overlays. On an interactive
avatar it also sets the native `disabled`. `isDisabled` is its deprecated name and still works.

<Example of={AvatarStories.States} caption="Hover, focus, and press are pinned for reference; the last row is live." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Image loading">

Avatar handles a broken image itself: when the photo fails to load, the fallback artwork takes its
place, and a new `src` is tried again. You don't need to check the URL first.

<Example of={AvatarStories.Content} caption="A photo, no photo, a failed photo, a long name with a badge, and a hand-built group." />

</Subsection>

<Dont caption="Rely on the photo alone. The avatar has no accessible name, and a failed photo leaves an unnamed silhouette." code={`<Avatar src={photo} />`} />
<Do caption="Let Avatar add the badge to the name and report the selection: this one reads “Ben Averill, busy”, a pressed toggle button." code={`<Avatar src={photo} name="Ben Averill" presence="busy" isSelected isInteractive onClick={select} />`}>
  <Avatar src={photoB} name="Ben Averill" presence="busy" isSelected isInteractive onClick={() => undefined} />
</Do>
<Dont caption="Write the state into name yourself. It's read twice." code={`<Avatar name="Ben Averill, busy" presence="busy" />`} />
<Do caption="Mark an avatar decorative when a visible name sits next to it." code={sample1}>
  <Avatar src={photoC} decorative />
  <span>Ben Averill</span>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Set decorative on an interactive avatar to skip its name. It's ignored, and without a name the button is unnamed." code={`<Avatar src={photo} decorative isInteractive onClick={select} />`} />
</Guidelines>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- A static avatar is a `span` with `role="img"`; an interactive one is a native `button` with
  `type="button"`. The photo is `alt=""` and hidden, so the root carries the name.
- The name comes from `aria-labelledby`, else `aria-label`, `name`, or `alt`, in that order. Avatar
  adds the badge after it, as in "Ben Averill, busy". With `aria-labelledby` it adds nothing.
- The added words are props with English defaults, so they can be translated: `getPresenceLabel`
  ("online", "offline", "busy"), `getStatusLabel` ("accepted", "declined"), and `selectedLabel`
  ("selected").
- In development, Avatar warns in the console when an avatar that isn't decorative has no name.
- `decorative` hides a static avatar from assistive technology. It's ignored on an interactive
  avatar.
- An interactive avatar given `isSelected` is a toggle button: it sets `aria-pressed`, which is
  announced even with `aria-labelledby`, and "selected" isn't added to its name. Without `isSelected`
  it has no `aria-pressed`. A static avatar can't be pressed, so a selected one adds `selectedLabel`
  to its name: "Ben Averill, selected, busy".
- The badges differ in shape as well as color: dot, ring, bar, check, and cross.
- `disabled` sets the native `disabled` on an interactive avatar, so it leaves the tab order. On a
  static avatar it only dims it.
- Focus shows the shared Focus Ring, 2px outside the circle.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to an interactive avatar. A disabled one, or a static one, is skipped.</Key>
  <Key keys="Enter">Activates an interactive avatar.</Key>
  <Key keys="Space">Activates an interactive avatar.</Key>
</Keyboard>
