<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- An unread or pending count beside a label or an icon.
- A small total in navigation, a list, a table, or a card.
- A change in a score, such as `+1` or `-1`.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **A status or a category in words:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Label" />. Badge holds one to four characters.
- **A reference to a record:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Tag" />, which can link to it.
- **An action:** <DocLink to="Components/Actions/Button" />. Badge isn't focusable and takes no click handler.
- **Progress or loading:** <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Progress Bar" /> or <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Spinner" />.

</UseInstead>
</Usage>

<Section title="Variants">

`tone` sets the fill. `default` is a dark neutral fill, `brand` the brand color, and `success` and
`error` show a change up or down. Every tone sets its text in the inverse content color.

<Example of={BadgeStories.Variants} />

<Subsection title="Size">

Badge has one size: 16px tall and at least 16px wide, with 4px of padding at each side and its text
in `heading-xxs`. There's no `size` prop.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Content length">

Badge doesn't wrap or truncate its text, and it doesn't format numbers. A long value widens the badge,
and in a container too narrow for it, the badge overflows. Cap large counts yourself, for example at
`99+`.

<Example of={BadgeStories.EdgeCases} caption="Values longer than four characters, a narrow container, and a dark surface." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="In context">

Badge has no hover, focus, pressed, or disabled look: it reads the same wherever it sits. Put it next
to the words that say what it counts.

<Example of={BadgeStories.Content} caption="Counts and deltas on their own and inside cards." />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Do and don't">

<Guidelines>
<Do caption="Cap a large count so the badge stays one to four characters." code={`<Badge>{unreadCount > 99 ? '99+' : unreadCount}</Badge>`}>
  <Badge>99+</Badge>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Pass a raw count. Badge doesn't cap it, so a large number stretches the badge." code={`<Badge>{unreadCount}</Badge>`}>
  <Badge>184320</Badge>
</Dont>
<Do caption="Keep the sign in a change, so its direction doesn't depend on color." code={`<Badge tone="success">+2</Badge>
<Badge tone="error">-1</Badge>`}>
  <Badge tone="success">+2</Badge>
  <Badge tone="error">-1</Badge>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Show a change by tone alone. The colors aren't read out, and not everyone can tell them apart." code={`<Badge tone="success">2</Badge>
<Badge tone="error">1</Badge>`}>
  <Badge tone="success">2</Badge>
  <Badge tone="error">1</Badge>
</Dont>
<Do caption="Use Label for a status in words." code={`<Label tone="success">Active</Label>`}>
  <Label tone="success">Active</Label>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Put a word in a Badge, which is sized for a number and doesn't wrap." code={`<Badge tone="success">Active</Badge>`}>
  <Badge tone="success">Active</Badge>
</Dont>
</Guidelines>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Badge renders a `span` with no role. It isn't focusable and has no keyboard interaction.
- Its text is read in place, as part of the text around it. Put the words that say what the number
  counts next to it, such as "Unread messages" before the badge.
- When there are no such words, set `label`, such as "3 unread messages". Badge renders it as visually
  hidden text and hides the visible number, so screen readers read the words instead of the number.
- Keep the sign in a change (`+1`, `-1`). The `success` and `error` colors alone don't say which way
  it went.
- `label` isn't an `aria-label`: ARIA doesn't allow a name on an element with no role, so screen
  readers may ignore one. Don't pass `aria-label` to Badge. The deprecated `ariaLabel` prop now
  renders as `label` does.

</Section>
