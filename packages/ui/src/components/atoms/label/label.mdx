<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- The status of a record: Draft, Active, At risk, Blocked, Complete.
- A category or a classification on a card, a table row, or a list item.
- Whether an item belongs to law or wealth, with `tone="law"` or `tone="wealth"`.
- A short qualifier inside a Text Field, after the input.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **Naming a form control:** <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Field" />, whose `label` is a real `label` element tied to the control.
- **A count or a change in a number:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Badge" />.
- **A reference that links to a record, or that people can remove:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Tag" />.
- **A filter people apply or change:** <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Chip" />.

</UseInstead>
</Usage>

<Section title="Variants">

`tone` says what the label means; `emphasis` sets how much it stands out. They're separate props, and
every tone comes in both emphases. `default` is neutral. `information`, `warning`, `error`, and
`success` are statuses, and their `subtle` text takes the status color. `law` and `wealth` mark an
item's product category, in the brand blue and in green.

`subtle`, the default, is a tinted fill for most labels. `bold` is a solid fill, for a label that has
to stand out in a busy row. Choose the emphasis for the layout, not to change the meaning.

<Example of={LabelStories.Variants} />

<Subsection title="Sizes">

`md`, the default, is 24px tall: its `heading-xs` line height sets the height, with no block padding
and 8px of padding at each side. `sm` is 20px tall, with `heading-xxs`, 2px of block padding, and 4px
at each side, for dense tables and compact cards. Corners are 4px at both sizes.

<Example of={LabelStories.Sizes} />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Text and length">

Label renders its text exactly as you pass it, with no change of case. It doesn't wrap and has no
ellipsis, so text longer than a few words overflows its container rather than shortening. Keep it to
one to three words.

<Example of={LabelStories.EdgeCases} caption="Long text in a narrow container, several labels wrapping in a row, and a dark surface." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Not interactive">

Label has no hover, focus, pressed, or disabled look. It's information about an item, read alongside
it.

<Example of={LabelStories.Content} caption="Labels in a table, a card, and a row." />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Do and don't">

<Guidelines>
<Do caption="Keep the text to a status or a category name." code={`<Label tone="warning">At risk</Label>`}>
  <Label tone="warning">At risk</Label>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Write a sentence in a Label. It doesn't wrap, so in a narrow space it runs past its container." code={`<Label tone="warning">Needs review before the filing deadline</Label>`}>
  <Label tone="warning">Needs review before the filing deadline</Label>
</Dont>
<Do caption="Use Tag for a value that links to a record or that people can remove." code={`<Tag href="/trusts/123" tone="green">Averill Family Living Trust</Tag>`}>
  <Tag href="#" tone="green">Averill Family Living Trust</Tag>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Add a click handler to a Label. It isn't focusable, so keyboard users can't reach it." code={`<Label tone="information" onClick={openTrust}>Averill Family Living Trust</Label>`} />
<Do caption="Name a form control with Field, whose label is tied to the control." code={`<Field label="Matter name">
    <TextField />

  <Field label="Matter name">
    <TextField />
  </Field>
</Do>
<Dont caption="Use Label above a control as its name. It isn't a form label, so the control stays unnamed." code={`<Label>Matter name</Label>
<TextField />`} />
</Guidelines>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Label renders a `span` with no role, so its text is read in place, as part of the content around it.
  It isn't focusable and has no keyboard interaction.
- It isn't a form label: it isn't tied to a control, and clicking it focuses nothing. To name a
  control, use <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Field" />.
- The text carries the meaning. Write the status in words; the tone's color supports it but isn't
  read out.
- Other attributes you pass go to the span, so a label you make clickable is still a `span` that
  keyboard users can't reach. Use a real control instead.

</Section>
