- Onboarding and guided setup, where the position matters more than the step names.
- A short multi-step form or dialog.
- The position in a carousel, one dot per slide.

</UseFor>

<Section title="Variants">

`appearance` sets the selected dot's color. `default` is the neutral bold color and `primary` the
brand color. `inverse` uses the neutral subtle color, for a bold surface such as
`color-background-neutral-bold-default`: in the light theme, a light dot on a dark surface. On the
page's own surface, in either theme, its selected dot almost disappears, and in the dark theme
`default` already reads. The unselected dots are outlined in every appearance.

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Position">

Progress Indicator is controlled: it shows `currentStep` of `totalSteps` and keeps no state of its
own. `totalSteps` sets the number of dots, at least one. `currentStep` counts from 1 and is clamped
to the range, so 0 shows the first step and a number past the end shows the last. Back and Next
buttons belong to the surrounding flow, not to the indicator.

<Example of={ProgressIndicatorStories.States} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Choosing a step">

Pass `onStepChange` to let people jump to a step. Each dot becomes a button that calls it with its
step number, with a hover and press fill and the shared Focus Ring; update `currentStep` in
response. The indicator is then a group of buttons rather than a progressbar, so assistive
technology reaches every dot. Without it, the dots are decorative and can't be focused. Add it only when jumping to any
step makes sense, such as in a carousel.

<Example of={ProgressIndicatorStories.Content} caption="Different totals, custom value text, compositions, and Back and Next buttons that own the step." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Narrow spaces">

The dots never wrap. The indicator is as wide as its dots, so in a narrow container let the layout
around it scroll or give it room.

<Example of={ProgressIndicatorStories.EdgeCases} />

</Subsection>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Without `onStepChange`, the root is a `div` with `role="progressbar"`, `aria-valuemin="1"`,
  `aria-valuemax` set to the number of steps, and `aria-valuenow` set to the current step. Pass
  `role` to change the role.
- `aria-valuetext` is "Step 2 of 5" by default. Pass `getValueText` when the journey has its own
  words, such as "Section 2 of 4"; it wins over an `aria-valuetext` you pass.
- The name comes from `aria-label`, then `aria-labelledby`, then `label`. Name the journey, such as
  "Client onboarding".
- Without `onStepChange`, the dots are hidden from assistive technology and the indicator isn't in
  the tab order.
- With `onStepChange`, the root is a `group` with the same name and no value, since a progressbar's
  children are presentational and its buttons might not be exposed. Each dot is a native `button`
  named by `getStepLabel`, "Go to step 3" by default, and the current one has
  `aria-current="step"`. Translate `getStepLabel` with the rest of your interface.
- The current dot is filled and the others are outlined, so position doesn't depend on color alone.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">With `onStepChange`, moves focus to each dot in turn. Without it, the indicator is skipped.</Key>
  <Key keys="Enter">Goes to the focused dot's step.</Key>
  <Key keys="Space">Goes to the focused dot's step.</Key>
</Keyboard>
