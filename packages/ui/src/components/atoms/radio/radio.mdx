<Subsection title="Radio Group">

Radio Group is a native `fieldset` that holds, in order:

1. The `legend`, from `label`, with a `*` after it when `required` is set.
2. The `description`, when it's set.
3. The options: a Radio for each entry in `options`, or your `children`.
4. The `errorMessage`, when it's set.

</Subsection>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="States">

Selected and unselected come from `checked` or `defaultChecked`, or from the group's `value`.
`invalid` draws the circle in the error color and changes nothing else. `disabled` draws the circle
and the label in the disabled color, as Checkbox does.

<Example of={RadioStories.States} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Radio Group">

Radios that share a `name` are one choice: selecting one clears the others. Pass `options` and Radio
Group renders a Radio for each, gives them one `name`, and keeps the selected value in `value` or
`defaultValue`. Pass `children` instead for a layout of your own: give those radios one `name` and
set their checked values and other states yourself. The group's `disabled` and `required` still
reach them, so a disabled group's radios look disabled too.

Options stack by default; `orientation="horizontal"` sets them in a row that wraps, with space
between the options. `required` makes every radio in the group natively required, so the browser
asks for a choice, and puts the `*` after the legend rather than after each option.

<Example of={RadioStories.Content} caption="Content: label text, required, a radio named by aria-label, a long label, and groups from options and from children, controlled, horizontal, required, and disabled from options and from children." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Controlled or uncontrolled">

On Radio Group, pass `value` with `onValueChange` to keep the choice in your state, or
`defaultValue` to let the radios keep it. `onValueChange` gets the selected option's value. A single
Radio takes `checked` or `defaultChecked`, and its `onCheckedChange` fires only when it becomes
selected, not when another radio takes over.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Labels">

The circle sits before the label and is centered on it, so when a label wraps to several lines the
circle lines up with the middle of the text, not the first line. Keep option labels short.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Focus and hover">

Hover and press show a round overlay behind the circle, in the brand color when it's selected and in
a neutral color otherwise. Keyboard focus shows the same overlay and adds the shared Focus Ring
around the circle, as on Checkbox.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Radio renders a native `input type="radio"` inside a `label`, so it's focusable, announced as a
  radio button, and named by the label text. Don't rebuild it with `role="radio"` on another element.
- Without a visible label, name it with `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. Nothing warns you when
  both are missing.
- Radios that share a `name` are one group to the keyboard: Tab stops on the group once, and the
  arrow keys move the selection. Radio Group shares the name for options; set it on `children`
  yourself.
- Radio Group is a `fieldset` named by its `legend`, so assistive technology can read the question
  with the options. Its `description` and `errorMessage` are added to the description of the
  fieldset and of each radio from `options`.
- `invalid` sets `aria-invalid` on the radio, or on the fieldset and each radio from `options`.
- `disabled` and `required` are the native attributes: a disabled radio leaves the tab order, and a
  required one is announced as required. The `*` is hidden from assistive technology, so it isn't
  read as part of the name. A required Radio Group makes each of its radios natively required.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus into the group, to the selected radio, or to the first one when none is selected. The next Tab leaves the group.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Down">Selects the next radio in the group, wrapping to the first. Arrow Right does the same.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Up">Selects the previous radio in the group, wrapping to the last. Arrow Left does the same.</Key>
  <Key keys="Space">Selects the focused radio.</Key>
</Keyboard>
