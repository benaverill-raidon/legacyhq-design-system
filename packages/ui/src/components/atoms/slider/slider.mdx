  Slider sets a number by moving a handle along a track, when where the value sits in its range

<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- A setting people adjust by feel, such as volume or zoom, with Slider.
- An adjustment either side of a neutral middle, such as an offset, with Slider Centered.
- A low and a high bound, such as a price or date filter, with Slider Range.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **An exact number:** <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Text Field" />. Dragging to a precise value is slow.
- **A few named options:** <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Radio" />.
- **Showing progress, not setting a value:** <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Progress Bar" />.

</UseInstead>
</Usage>

<Section title="Anatomy">

<Anatomy specimen={<Slider label="Notification volume" defaultValue={60} showSteps step={20} showValue />}>
  <Part name="label" target={sliderStyles.label}>`label`, a native `label` for the input. Optional, but then `aria-label` is required.</Part>
  <Part name="track" target={sliderStyles.track}>The full range, on either side of the handle. Its thickness is set by `size`.</Part>
  <Part name="active track" target={sliderStyles.activeTrack}>The filled part: from `min` to the value, from zero on Slider Centered, or between the handles on Slider Range.</Part>
  <Part name="steps" target={sliderStyles.stepDot}>Dots at `min` and `max`, always, and at each step with `showSteps`. Decorative.</Part>
  <Part name="handle" target={sliderStyles.handleVisual}>The bar at the value, which you drag. Slider Range has two.</Part>
  <Part name="value indicator" target={sliderStyles.valueIndicator}>A bubble with the value above the handle. Shown on hover, focus, and drag, or always with `showValue`.</Part>
  <Part name="input" target={sliderStyles.input}>The native range input, transparent, over the whole control. It holds the value, focus, and keyboard; the control around it handles the pointer.</Part>
</Anatomy>

<Section title="Variants">

Three components share one look. Slider fills from `min` to the value. Slider Centered fills from
zero to the value, either way, and its range defaults to -100 to 100. Slider Range has two handles
and fills between them. All three can run `horizontal` or `vertical`; a vertical slider is a fixed
104px tall, with the lowest value at the bottom.

<Example of={SliderStories.Variants} />

<Subsection title="Sizes">

`size` sets only the track's thickness: `xs` is 16px, `sm` 24px, and `md`, the default, 40px. The
handle and the step dots are the same at every size, so the handle lands on the end dots at `min`
and `max` whatever the size.

<Example of={SliderStories.Sizes} />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Steps">

The value snaps to `step`. Dots always mark `min` and `max`. `showSteps` adds a dot at each step
between them, worked out from `step` when that gives 25 values or fewer, or placed at the values in
`steps`. `steps` only places dots, and only with `showSteps`: the handle still snaps to `step`, so
keep the two in line.

<Example of={SliderStories.Content} caption="Content: steps from step and from a list, the value bubble on hover and with showValue, a custom range, and sliders in cards." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The value bubble">

The bubble above the handle shows the value on hover, keyboard focus, and while dragging. Set
`showValue` to keep it showing, when people need to read the number without touching the slider.
A slider with `showValue` makes room for the bubble between the label and the track, so the two
never overlap. Without it, the bubble shows over whatever is above while you use the slider. On
Slider Range both values show.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Two handles">

On Slider Range, the values stay at least `minDistance` apart, 1 by default; set it to `0` to let
the handles meet. A handle moved past the other trades places with it, so the lower value always
comes first. Set `disableSwap` to stop a handle at the other one instead.

In a form, the lower value submits under `name` and the upper under `name` with `Max` added, such as
`budget` and `budgetMax`. Set `minName` and `maxName` to submit them under other names.

<Example of={SliderStories.EdgeCases} caption="Edge cases: handles that meet, a minimum distance with and without swapping, a narrow container, short vertical sliders, and a dark surface." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Errors and Field">

`invalid` draws the track and the handle in the error colors and sets `aria-invalid` on the handle,
or on both handles of Slider Range. Pair it with a message that says what to change.

Field wraps a Slider like any other form control: leave out the Slider's own `label` and let Field's
label name it, its `message` describe it, `required` show the `*`, and `state="error"` mark it. Field
passes `aria-invalid` for an error, and Slider shows the error look for that too, as Select and the
pickers do. On Slider Range, Field's label names the group and starts each handle's name; its `id`
goes to the lower handle, so clicking the label focuses it; and the message, the error, and
`required` go to both handles. A slider always has a value, so `required` only adds the `*`: the
browser never stops a submit for it.

<Example of={SliderStories.InField} caption="A Slider with Field's helper text, and a required Slider Range with Field's error." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Pointer and focus">

Press anywhere on the track and the handle jumps there; on Slider Range, the nearer handle does.
Then drag. Pressing or dragging moves focus to the input without a focus ring; the ring around the
handle shows only for keyboard focus. It's the shared Focus Ring, drawn around the handle rather
than the input, which fills the whole control. Hover and press change the handle's color, and pressing narrows it.

<Example of={SliderStories.States} />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Slider renders a native `input type="range"`, so it's announced as a slider with its value, and the
  keyboard works with no extra code. `label` is a native `label` for it; without one, pass
  `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`, which go to the input.
- Slider Range renders two range inputs inside a `group`. The range's name comes from `label`, or
  from `aria-labelledby` or `aria-label` when there's no visible label; it names the group, and each
  handle is named by it and then its own `minLabel` or `maxLabel` ("Minimum value" and "Maximum
  value" by default, so pass translations). `aria-describedby`, `aria-invalid`, and `required` go to
  both handles, since each is focused on its own; other attributes go to the root `div`.
- `invalid` sets `aria-invalid` on the handle (both, on Slider Range), and an `aria-invalid` passed
  in shows the error look too. The color isn't the message: pair it with text, such as Field's error
  message, which Field connects with `aria-describedby`.
- The track, steps, and drawn handle are hidden from assistive technology. The value bubble is a
  native `output` linked to its input.
- `disabled` is the native attribute: the slider leaves the tab order and ignores the pointer.
- Focus shows as the shared Focus Ring around the handle, for keyboard focus only.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to the slider. On Slider Range, to the lower handle, then the upper. A disabled slider is skipped.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Right">Raises the value by one `step`. Arrow Up does the same.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Left">Lowers the value by one `step`. Arrow Down does the same.</Key>
  <Key keys="Home">Sets the value to `min`.</Key>
  <Key keys="End">Sets the value to `max`.</Key>
  <Key keys="Page Up">Raises the value by a larger amount, set by the browser. Page Down lowers it.</Key>
</Keyboard>

</Section>
