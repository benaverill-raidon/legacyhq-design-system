<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- A short status beside what it's about: a sync state, a save confirmation, a review count.
- A status that sometimes needs one more sentence, opened on click with `content`.
- A summary line for a form or a section, such as how many items need attention.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **A hint on hover or focus:** <DocLink to="Components/Overlays/Tooltip" />. Inline Message opens only on click.
- **A message about one form control:** <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Field" />'s `message`, which is tied to the control for assistive technology.
- **Something people must read, or act on:** <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Section Message" />, which shows its description and actions without a click.
- **A page-level or passing announcement:** <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Banner" /> or <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Toast" />.

</UseInstead>
</Usage>

<Section title="Variants">

`tone` sets the status: `default`, `information`, `success`, `warning`, or `error`. It picks the
icon and its color, and the tint the row shows on hover, on focus, and while its popup is open; a
row without `content` has no tint. There's no status icon for `default`, so it shows a plain dot.
`info`, the earlier name for `information`, is still accepted.

<Example of={InlineMessageStories.Variants} />

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Opening the detail">

With `content`, clicking the row, or pressing Enter or Space on it, opens a popup below it. Clicking
the row again, pressing Escape, or clicking outside closes it. It never opens on hover. Without
`content`, the row is plain text: no button, no popup.

<Example of={InlineMessageStories.Content} caption="Title only, title and secondary text, no content, and inside a card." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Controlled or uncontrolled">

Inline Message keeps its own open state, starting from `defaultOpen`. To control it, pass `open`
and update it from `onOpenChange`, which is called on every click of the row and with `false` when
Escape or a click outside closes the popup. Use that to open the detail right after an action
finishes, for example.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Placement and length">

The popup opens below the row, lined up with its start edge, and moves to whichever side fits best
near the edge of the window. It's as wide as its content and doesn't wrap, and neither do the title
and secondary text, so keep all three short.

<Example of={InlineMessageStories.EdgeCases} caption="Rows at the window's edges, a long detail that runs as one line, and a dark surface." />

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- With `content`, the row is a native `button` with `type="button"`: focusable, and activated by
  Enter and Space. Keyboard focus shows the shared Focus Ring with the same tint as hover.
- The button's accessible name is its title and secondary text. Don't put links or other controls
  in them, since they'd be nested inside a button.
- Popup sets `aria-expanded` on the button, and `aria-controls` pointing at the popup while it's
  open. The popup has no role of its own.
- Focus stays on the button when the popup opens, and Escape closes it. The popup is rendered at the
  end of the page, so Tab moves on through the page rather than into it.
- The status icon and the `default` dot are hidden from assistive technology, and Inline Message
  adds no hidden status text, unlike Section Message. The title has to carry the status in words.
- Without `content`, the row is a plain `div` that isn't focusable.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to the row when it has `content`. A row without it is skipped.</Key>
  <Key keys="Enter">Opens or closes the popup.</Key>
  <Key keys="Space">Opens or closes the popup.</Key>
  <Key keys="Escape">Closes the popup. Focus stays on the row.</Key>
</Keyboard>

</Section>
