<Section title="Variants">

`size` and `context` are separate props, and they work the same way as Text Field's.

<Example of={TextAreaStories.Variants} />

<Subsection title="Sizes">

`size` sets the padding, corner radius, and text size. `sm` and `md` use the body text; `lg` uses
the larger body text, with more padding and rounder corners. Figma draws `md` and `lg`; `sm` is a
code-only option for compact key and value rows. The height comes from `rows`, or from `autoResize`,
which starts each size at the control height: 32px, 40px, or 48px.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Context">

`default` is the bordered field for forms. `inline` is for a field inside a row of other content,
such as a Key Value Pair value: it has no border or background until someone hovers it, then shows
the hover background and a line along the bottom. Focus and invalid show a thicker line in the focus
or error color, and the bottom corners are always square.

Field passes `context="inline"` to its control when the Field is inline. The older `appearance`
prop still works: `subtle` and `inline` both mean `context="inline"`.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="States">

There's no `state` prop. Hover, focus, and autofill come from the browser; `invalid` and `disabled`
are props. Focus changes the border to the focus color and thickens it on the inside, so the text
never moves. Hover shows only while the field isn't focused, and an invalid field keeps its error
border through hover and focus.

Autofill works as it does in Text Field: a `default` field shows the autofill tint over its surface,
hovering replaces it, and focus and invalid keep it. An `inline` field shows the tint only while
it's focused. The browser's own autofill color is hidden, so the field looks the same in every
browser.

<Example of={TextAreaStories.States} caption="Hover, focus, and autofill are pinned for this reference; invalid and disabled are the real props." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Resizing">

`resize` sets which way someone can drag the field: `vertical` (the default), `horizontal`, `both`,
or `none`. Vertical lets the field grow taller without changing its width, so a drag can't push it
out of its column. Use `none` for a fixed layout; long text still scrolls. A disabled field can't be
resized.

<Example of={TextAreaStories.Resize} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Growing with content">

`autoResize` makes the field grow as text is added and shrink as it's removed. It starts at the
control height for its size and stops growing at `maxRows` lines, 6 unless you set it, then scrolls.
It replaces `rows` and the drag handle, and it measures again when the field's width changes.

For a value edited in place, put `<TextArea autoResize context="inline" />` inside an Inline Edit
with `actionPlacement="end"` and `actionAlignment="start"`. Inline Edit leaves Enter for new lines:
Ctrl+Enter or Cmd+Enter confirms, and Escape cancels.

<Example of={TextAreaStories.AutoResize} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Trailing icon">

`iconAfter` puts an icon or a control at the end of the frame, pinned to the top so it stays put as
the text grows. It isn't hidden from assistive technology, so an Icon Button there keeps its own
name and tab stop.

<Example of={TextAreaStories.IconAfter} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Labels and counts">

Text Area has no label, helper text, or character count of its own. Wrap it in Field for the label
and a message; a count is text you show beside it.

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Text Area renders a native `textarea` inside a `div` frame, so it's focusable and announced as a
  multi-line text field. Typing, wrapping, scrolling, autofill, and spellcheck work with no extra
  code.
- It has no label of its own. Wrap it in Field, which wires a native `label` to it, or pass
  `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. A placeholder isn't a label.
- `invalid` sets `aria-invalid` on the textarea. In a Field, the error message is added to its
  description, so the error has words as well as a color.
- `disabled` is the native attribute: the textarea leaves the tab order and can't be edited or
  resized.
- `iconAfter` isn't hidden, so a control there is its own tab stop after the textarea, with its own
  accessible name.
- Focus shows on the frame's own border, in the focus color, rather than as the shared Focus Ring
  around it.

Text Area adds no keys of its own; the native textarea handles typing and editing.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to the textarea, then to any control in `iconAfter`. A disabled field is skipped.</Key>
  <Key keys="Enter">Starts a new line.</Key>
</Keyboard>
