<Section title="Variants">

`size` and `context` are separate props, and every pairing is designed.

<Example of={TextFieldStories.Variants} />

<Subsection title="Sizes">

Three heights: `sm` (32px), `md` (40px), and `lg` (48px), shared with Button, Select, and the other
controls so they line up in a row. `lg` also sets the text one type size larger, with more padding
and rounder corners.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Context">

`context` says where the field sits. `default` is the bordered field for forms. `inline` is for a
field inside a row of other content, such as a Key Value Pair value or a search box in a menu: it has
no border or background until someone hovers it, then shows the hover background and a line along
the bottom. Focus and invalid show a thicker line in the focus or error color, and the bottom corners
are always square.

Field passes `context="inline"` to its control when the Field is inline. Inside a Table's editable
cell, Text Field takes the cell's size and the inline context, whatever you pass. The older
`appearance` prop still works: `subtle` and `inline` both mean `context="inline"`.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="States">

There's no `state` prop. Hover, focus, and autofill come from the browser; `invalid` and `disabled`
are props. Focus changes the border to the focus color and thickens it on the inside, so the text
never moves. Hover shows only while the field isn't focused. An invalid field keeps its thicker error
border whether or not it has focus, and hovering doesn't hide it.

<Example of={TextFieldStories.States} caption="Hover, focus, and autofill are pinned for this reference; invalid and disabled are the real props." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Autofill">

When the browser fills in a saved value, a `default` field shows the autofill tint over its surface.
Hovering replaces the tint with the usual hover background; focus and invalid keep it. An `inline`
field stays clear at rest and shows the tint only while it's focused. The browser's own autofill
color is hidden, so the field looks the same in every browser.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Before and after the text">

`iconBefore` holds an icon or a short text prefix, such as `$`. It's decorative and hidden from
assistive technology, so don't put anything there that the label doesn't already say. A text prefix
uses the larger body text at every size.

`iconAfter` holds an icon, a Label for a unit, or a real control: an Icon Button with
`appearance="subtle"` and `shape="square"` to clear the field, or a Button when the action needs a
word. It isn't hidden, so a control there keeps its own name and tab stop.

`leadingContent` sits between the two and can hold controls. Select uses it for its multi-select
chips. It shrinks and clips so the field stays on one line.

<Example of={TextFieldStories.Content} caption="Content: placeholder and value, an icon and a text prefix, a clear action, a unit, and a native label." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Width">

Text Field fills the width of its container, so set the width on the container. A value longer than
the field scrolls inside the input.

<Example of={TextFieldStories.EdgeCases} caption="Edge cases: a long value, a full-width field, real browser autofill to try, and a dark surface." />

</Subsection>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Text Field renders a native `input` inside a `div` frame, so it's focusable and announced as a text
  field, or as the role its `type` gives it, such as a search box. Typing, autofill, spellcheck, and
  copy and paste work with no extra code.
- It has no label of its own. Wrap it in Field, which wires a native `label` to it, or pass
  `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`. A placeholder isn't a label.
- `invalid` sets `aria-invalid` on the input. In a Field, the error message is added to the input's
  description, so the error has words as well as a color.
- `disabled` is the native attribute: the input leaves the tab order and can't be edited.
- `iconBefore` is hidden from assistive technology. `iconAfter` isn't, so a control there is its own
  tab stop after the input, with its own accessible name.
- Focus shows on the field's own border, in the focus color, rather than as the shared Focus Ring
  around it.

Text Field adds no keys of its own; the native input handles typing and editing.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to the input, then to any control in `iconAfter`. A disabled field is skipped.</Key>
</Keyboard>

</Section>
