<Section title="When to use">

<Usage>
<UseFor>

- A summary, list, form, or dashboard section that should read as one group.
- An inset or nested panel, with a different `surface`.
- Content that runs to the edges, such as an image above text, with rounded corners.

</UseFor>
<UseInstead>

- **Layout with no surface, border, or clipping:** plain layout markup. Card adds nothing there.
- **A message about the page or a task:** <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Section Message" /> or <DocLink to="Components/Feedback/Banner" />.
- **Content above the page that needs a response:** <DocLink to="Components/Overlays/Modal Dialog" />.
- **Many records compared side by side:** <DocLink to="Components/Data Display/Table" />.

</UseInstead>
</Usage>

<Section title="Variants">

`surface`, `border`, and `borderRadius` are independent, so any surface takes any radius, with or
without a border. Figma has all 30 combinations.

<Subsection title="Surface">

`surface` picks one of the elevation surface colors, which follow the theme. `raised` (the default)
is the card resting on the page, `default` is the page's own color, and `sunken` and `deep` sit
below it, for inset panels. `none` is transparent, so the background behind the card shows through.
No surface adds a shadow: a raised card is set apart by its color and its border.

<Example of={CardStories.AllVariants} caption="Every surface at every radius, with and without a border." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Border">

`border` is on by default: a 1px line in the default border color. `border={false}` removes it
completely, so the card doesn't keep an invisible 1px. With `surface="none"`, the border alone
outlines the content; without either, the card only rounds and clips it. Children keep full opacity
whatever the surface.

<Example of={CardStories.NoSurface} caption="A transparent card with and without its border." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Corners">

`borderRadius` is `lg` (8px, the default), `xl` (12px), or `xxl` (16px). Content is clipped to it.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Content">

Card renders `children` directly, with no wrapper, so the content decides its own padding, gap,
typography, and layout. It can be several elements, composed components, or another card.

<Example of={CardStories.ContentSlot} caption="A summary with a badge and metrics, and a sunken checklist with a nested card." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Clipping">

The card hides overflow at its rounded edge, so content can run to the edges and still get the
corners. Keep focusable controls far enough in from the edge for their focus ring to show, and use a
portaled overlay, such as <DocLink to="Components/Overlays/Popup" /> or a picker, for anything that
has to extend past the card.

<Example of={CardStories.EdgeToEdge} caption="A banner that runs to the edges, above padded text." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Size">

Card has no size of its own. It fills the width its layout gives it and grows with its content, and
it can shrink inside a flex or grid layout. Set a width or height on it with `className` or `style`
when you need one. An empty card is only as tall as its border.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Interactive content">

Card has no hover, pressed, selected, or disabled state, and adds no event handling of its own.
It passes the HTML attributes and handlers you give it, such as `style`, `data-*`, or `onClick`, to
its `div` unchanged, but a handler there doesn't make the card a control: it gets no role, no tab
stop, and no keyboard activation. For a card people can open, put a link or button inside it, as in
Do and don't below. Controls inside it, like the form here, keep their own behavior.

<Example of={CardStories.WithForm} caption="A form inside a card, with its own submit button." />

</Subsection>

</Section>

</Card>`}
>
  <Card className={storyStyles.example}>
    <h3 className={storyStyles.heading}>Project overview</h3>
    <p className={storyStyles.description}>The content brings its own padding.</p>
  </Card>
</Dont>
<Do
  caption="Put a real link or button inside the card for its action."
  code={`<Card>
    <section className={styles.content}>
      <h3>Community projects</h3>
      <Button>View projects</Button>
    </section>
</Card>`}
>
  <Card className={storyStyles.example}>
    <section className={storyStyles.content}>
      <h3 className={storyStyles.heading}>Community projects</h3>
      <div>
        <Button>View projects</Button>
      </div>
    </section>
  </Card>
</Do>
<Dont
  caption="Make the whole card a click target. It's a div: keyboard users can't reach it, and it isn't announced as a link or button."
  code={`<Card onClick={() => navigate('/projects')}>
    <h3>Community projects</h3>
</Card>`}
/>
<Do
  caption="Keep the border on a raised card that sits on the page. Its border is what sets it apart."
  code={`<Card surface="raised">…</Card>`}
/>
<Dont
  caption="Remove the border from a raised card on the page and expect a shadow to separate it. Raised adds no shadow."
  code={`<Card surface="raised" border={false}>…</Card>`}
/>
</Guidelines>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- Card renders a plain `div`: no role, no landmark, no tab stop, and no keyboard behavior, even
  with an `onClick` passed to it. Screen readers don't announce it, and it doesn't change how its
  content is read.
- The content brings the semantics. Use headings, and a `section` or `article` named by its heading,
  as the examples do, when the card holds a group people should find.
- Controls inside keep their own focus and keyboard behavior. The card clips at its edge, so keep
  them inset enough for the focus ring to show.
- Surface and border are visual only. Don't rely on a card's color to say something the content
  doesn't.

</Section>
