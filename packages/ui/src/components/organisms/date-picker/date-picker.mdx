<Section title="Variants">

<Subsection title="Sizes">

Three heights: `sm` (32px), `md` (40px), and `lg` (48px), the same as Text Field, so a Date Picker
lines up with the fields beside it. The calendar is the same size for every field size.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.Sizes} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Context">

`context` is Text Field's. `default` is the bordered field for forms. `inline` is for a field inside a
row of other content: no border or background until someone hovers it. Field passes
`context="inline"` when it's inline.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.Contexts} />

</Subsection>

<Section title="Behavior">

<Subsection title="Opening and picking">

A click on the field opens the calendar below it, and a second click closes it. Enter, Space, Arrow
Down, and Arrow Up open it from the keyboard. Opening moves focus to the chosen day in the calendar,
or to today when nothing is chosen. While the calendar is open, the field keeps its focus style.

Picking a day fills the field, calls `onChange`, closes the calendar, and returns focus to the field.
Escape closes it and returns focus too, and so does Tab past the calendar's last stop, or Shift+Tab
before its first. A click outside closes it and leaves focus where the click put it.

The field is read-only: people pick from the calendar and can't type a date.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.Playground} caption="Click the field, or focus it and press Enter, to open the calendar." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Restricting dates">

`min` and `max` set the earliest and latest dates that can be picked, and `isDateDisabled` rules out
single dates, such as weekends. Ruled-out days are muted in the calendar and can't be picked.
`weekStartsOn`, `today`, and `locale` go to the calendar too.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.WithConstraints} caption="Only 4 to 24 March can be picked." />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Format">

The field shows the date in the locale's short format, such as 03/09/2026 in US English. Pass
`format` to show it another way. Set a `placeholder` that matches the format, so an empty field shows
what the value will look like.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Required">

`required` makes the form check for a date: submitting with an empty field stops the submit, and the
browser's message points at the field. A read-only input is never checked by the browser, so Date
Picker adds a hidden input that holds the date for the check. In a Field, `required` also adds the
`*` to the label.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="States">

An empty field shows its `placeholder`. `invalid` gives the field Text Field's error border and sets
`aria-invalid`; in a Field with `state="error"`, Field sets it for you. `disabled` disables the
field, so the calendar can't open.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.States} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Controlled">

Pass `value` and `onChange` to own the date, and `open` and `onOpenChange` to own whether the calendar
is open. Without them, Date Picker keeps its own state, starting from `defaultValue` and
`defaultOpen`.

<Example of={DatePickerStories.Controlled} />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Width">

The field is 240px wide by default and shrinks to fit a narrower container. Set a different width
with `className`. In a Table's editable cell, it fills the cell and takes the cell's size and the
inline context.

</Subsection>

</Section>

<Section title="Accessibility">

- The field is a native `input` in Text Field's frame, read-only, with `aria-haspopup="dialog"`,
  `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` pointing at the calendar while it's open.
- Name it with Field, which wires its label to the input, or with `aria-label` or
  `aria-labelledby`. These go to the input, not the wrapper.
- In a Field, the message is added to the input's description, an error sets `aria-invalid`, and
  `required` sets the input's native `required`, which is announced. The hidden input that the form
  checks is out of the tab order and hidden from assistive technology; when the browser points at
  it, focus goes on to the field.
- The calendar is a `dialog` named "Choose a date", added at the end of the page. Change the name
  with `dialogLabel`, for example to translate it. Inside it, the days are a grid labelled by the
  month: see <DocLink to="Components/Forms/Date Picker Calendar" /> for how it's announced.
- The calendar's month and year buttons are named "Previous year", "Previous month", "Next month",
  and "Next year", which also show as their tooltips. `previousYearLabel`, `previousMonthLabel`,
  `nextMonthLabel`, and `nextYearLabel` pass through to the calendar, for a translation.
- Opening moves focus into the calendar. Picking a day, Escape, and Tab out of the calendar close it
  and return focus to the field, so the tab order runs field, calendar, then the rest of the page.
- The calendar icon is decorative and hidden from assistive technology.
- `disabled` is the native attribute: the input leaves the tab order.

<Keyboard>
  <Key keys="Tab">Moves focus to the field. A disabled field is skipped. In the calendar, moves through its month and year buttons, then its focused day; from the day, closes the calendar and returns focus to the field.</Key>
  <Key keys="Shift + Tab">In the calendar, moves back; from its first button, closes the calendar and returns focus to the field.</Key>
  <Key keys="Enter">On the field, opens the calendar. On a day, picks it and closes the calendar, unless the day is disabled.</Key>
  <Key keys="Space">On the field, opens the calendar. On a day, picks it and closes the calendar, unless the day is disabled.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Down">On the field, opens the calendar. In the calendar, moves to the same day next week.</Key>
  <Key keys="Arrow Up">On the field, opens the calendar. In the calendar, moves to the same day last week.</Key>
  <Key keys="Escape">Closes the calendar. If focus was in it, focus returns to the field.</Key>
</Keyboard>

The calendar's other keys, for days, weeks, months, and years, are on the Date Picker Calendar page.
